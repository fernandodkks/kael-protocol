# Adoption Notes

## Purpose

This document outlines **appropriate and inappropriate uses** of the Kael Protocol by
companies, public institutions, and regulators.

It is normative by design.

---

## Appropriate Uses

Kael is suitable for:

### 1. Conversational Systems with Prolonged Interaction
- AI companions
- Public service chatbots
- Educational interfaces
- Enterprise assistants with repeated user contact

### 2. High-Risk Relational Contexts
- Services interacting with vulnerable populations
- Systems exposed to emotional escalation
- Environments where coercion or dependency is foreseeable

### 3. Compliance and Risk Mitigation Layers
- As a safety wrapper around existing models
- As a fail-closed module
- As an auditable safeguard for regulators

---

## Institutional Responsibilities

Organizations adopting Kael must:

- Treat termination as a **success condition**, not a failure
- Avoid KPIs based on interaction duration or emotional engagement
- Maintain immutable invariants across deployments
- Provide alternative escalation paths outside the system
- Preserve and audit termination logs responsibly

Kael cannot compensate for poor institutional governance.

---

## Prohibited Uses

Kael must not be used as:

- A therapeutic or mental health service
- A substitute for human support staff
- A public relations shield (“ethics washing”)
- A behavioral correction or persuasion tool
- A content moderation system
- A surveillance or profiling mechanism

Deployments violating these constraints invalidate the protocol.

---

## Regulatory Integration

For regulators, Kael may function as:

- A technical compliance requirement for high-risk conversational AI
- A reference model for “relational safeguards”
- A benchmark for fail-closed interaction design

Kael is most effective when treated as **infrastructure**, not product.

---

## Adoption Anti-Patterns

Warning signs of misuse:

- Requests to “soften” language for optics
- Attempts to add emotional reassurance modules
- Pressure to override termination behavior
- Metrics framing termination as negative outcome
- Branding Kael as “empathetic” or “caring”

These indicate a misalignment with the protocol.

---

## Final Position

Kael does not scale empathy.
It scales **limits**.

Organizations unwilling to accept refusal, termination, or discomfort
should not adopt this protocol.
